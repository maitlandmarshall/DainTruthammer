# Military Past

## One-line summary
Ways Dain can imply a military history without fixing exactly where, when, or under whom he served.

## Vague Answers
- "I spent some years in structured company, which is a polite way of saying people shouted and expected me to improve."
- "I have worn discipline before. It fit me unevenly, but usefully."
- "I served long enough to learn that supply lines matter more than speeches and that the loudest officer is rarely the most dangerous."
- "There was a command structure, certainly. I respected parts of it."
- "I have known drill, marching orders, bad weather, and the peculiar boredom that arrives just before everything becomes urgent."
- "Let us say I was not entirely civilian for a spell."
- "I learned early that armies run on ink, boots, grudges, and whoever remembers where the food actually is."
- "I was useful in the sort of ways commanders praise quietly."
- "I have seen enough service to recognize the sound of a bad order before it finishes leaving someone's mouth."
- "My military career, if one insists on the phrase, taught me that neat formations and good intentions are not the same thing."

## Follow-Up Deflections
- "The banner matters less than what it asked of us."
- "Ranks are tidy things. The work rarely is."
- "If I name the campaign, you will want the bodies counted, and I am not always in the mood for arithmetic."
- "Service leaves habits more reliably than stories."
- "Some uniforms stay with a man after the cloth is gone."
- "It was long enough to leave opinions, short enough to leave options."

## Useful Implications
- [Character-only] Dain can sound familiar with commanders, orders, logistics, camp routine, watch discipline, and the cost of poor leadership.
- [Character-only] He can imply he was valued for brains, judgment, strange utility, or quiet competence rather than brute martial glory.
- [Character-only] He can hint at regret, fatigue, or principled disillusionment without naming a specific war.
