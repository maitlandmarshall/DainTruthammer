# Hobgoblin Campfire Distractions

## One-line summary
Short Truthammer tales Dain can tell a charmed hobgoblin leader to keep him happy, listening, and looking at Dain instead of the camp.

## The Captain Who Inspected Steam
"In the lower halls there was once a captain so disciplined that he inspected everything before breakfast. Boots, buckles, spearheads, door hinges, suspicious shadows. One morning he found steam rising from a cooking pot and demanded it stand in ranks.

The steam did not comply.

This offended him greatly, so he assigned two guards to watch it. By noon, half the hall had gathered to see whether the steam would desert its post. By supper, the captain declared victory, because not a single wisp had escaped without becoming air first.

That is command, you see. A lesser mind notices only whether orders are obeyed. A great leader knows when to declare success before breakfast gets cold."

## The Day The Ledger Forgot Us
"During my apprenticeship, I once served in a counting room where every visitor had to be recorded. Name, business, shoe condition, degree of nuisance. Very thorough. Very civilized. Completely unbearable.

One day the ledger vanished.

The clerks panicked. The guards panicked. The visitors panicked out of politeness. My master simply sat down, poured tea, and said, 'Excellent. For one blessed hour, nobody owes us anything and we owe nobody an explanation.'

Then he looked at me and said, 'Dain, remember this: when memory fails, manners must carry the load.'

So if anyone nearby seems uncertain, do not trouble yourself. Feed them, nod wisely, and let manners do the marching."

## My Mother's Quiet Room
"My mother could correct a room without raising her voice. A rare talent. She would stand in a doorway, fold her hands, and every argument suddenly remembered it had somewhere else to be.

Once, two cousins were shouting over inheritance, honor, and one very ugly brass lamp. My mother listened for a full minute, then said, 'The lamp is ugly enough to belong to both of you.'

They stopped fighting at once. Not because the matter was settled, mind you, but because everyone had to look at the lamp.

That is the secret of command: do not always strike the quarrel. Sometimes you point at the lamp."

## The Apprenticeship Of Two Wrong Teachers
"I trained under people who prized precision and under others who prized results. I kept both lessons and annoyed both camps.

The precision master said, 'A thing done imperfectly is not done.'

The results master said, 'A thing done late is not useful.'

Naturally, I tried to satisfy both and invented a door latch that opened perfectly, immediately, and only for people it personally approved of. This was considered a failure by the precision camp because the latch developed opinions. It was considered a failure by the results camp because one of its opinions was that my master should wait outside.

I considered it my first diplomatic success. Everyone agreed on something, even if that something was me being difficult."

## The Helmet Full Of Soup
"There was a guard in the old roadworks who lost his bowl and refused to admit it. A proud fellow. Terrible liar. He ate soup from his helmet for three days and called it tactical readiness.

On the fourth day, the cook asked why his helmet smelled of onions.

He said, 'Because I am prepared for siege.'

Now, was this wise? No. Was it sanitary? Also no. But did every guard in that post remember to keep track of their bowls afterward? Absolutely.

Sometimes a leader does not need perfect followers. Sometimes he needs one spectacular example of what not to do, and a spoon."
