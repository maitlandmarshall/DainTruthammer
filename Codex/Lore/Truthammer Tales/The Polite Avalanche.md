# The Polite Avalanche

## One-line summary
A story about a mother, a standing stone, and an avalanche that was rude enough to arrive but polite enough to choose a less tragic target.

## Story
"One winter a slope above the village gave a deep groan, and old miners started muttering that the snowpack meant trouble. So my mother went outside, put both hands on a standing stone, and said, very politely, 'Not today, if you please.' The avalanche came down anyway, but split around the shrine rock and buried only the tool shed and Cousin Dorrin's least favorite cart. Ever since then we say, 'Stone remembers. Best speak politely near a mountain.' It may ignore you, mind, but it does remember."
