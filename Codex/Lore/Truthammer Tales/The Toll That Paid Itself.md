# The Toll That Paid Itself

## One-line summary
A Truthammer campfire tale about a road toll, a hungry guard, and the wisdom of accepting breakfast before demanding tribute.

## Story
"There was once a lower mountain road where a very serious guard set up a toll gate with no gate, no charter, and no idea what a charter was. He had a spear, a stool, and the confidence of a king who had misplaced his crown.

First came a merchant, and the guard said, 'Pay the toll.'

The merchant said, 'What toll?'

The guard said, 'The one I have just invented.'

So the merchant paid one copper, because merchants respect invention when it is cheap.

Then came a priest, and the guard said, 'Pay the toll.'

The priest said, 'I carry only blessings.'

So the guard took one blessing, though he complained it was difficult to bite.

Then came a grandmother with a breakfast pot. The guard raised his spear and said, 'Pay the toll.'

The grandmother said, 'Sit down, fool boy. You cannot guard a road on an empty stomach.'

Now this was a legal argument of tremendous force, because it smelled of onions.

The guard sat. The merchant sat. The priest sat. Three miners sat. Two children sat. A goat sat without being invited, which is how goats attend most public functions.

By the time the pot was empty, everyone agreed the road had been protected from hunger, suspicion, and poor manners. The guard declared the toll paid in full. The grandmother declared the road free again. The goat declared nothing, but stole the stool.

And that, good friends, is why a shared fire is older than any toll gate. A road belongs to whoever can walk it without making enemies of every hungry soul upon it."

## Dainism Used
- "A shared fire is older than any toll gate."
- [Party] Useful when turning a demand for payment into a meal, a joke, and a temporary peace.
